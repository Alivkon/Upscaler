Demand check for themed collection page names

Question: which theme names, worded the way people actually search or ask an AI assistant, are worth a themed page like "dark academia" (which already brought ChatGPT visitors)? No tool publishes what people ask ChatGPT, so everything below is a proxy: whether big wallpaper sites/apps bothered to build a dedicated category for the phrase, how many items are in it, Etsy listing presence, and Reddit activity. Numbers from SEO blogs are marked as such and treated as weak.

Conclusions

  • My original claim about "stormy ocean wallpaper" did not hold up. Across every source checked — dedicated-category presence, Reddit search, Reddit browsing — it produced the weakest or no signal of any theme tested. I was wrong to assert it as commonly asked for; I'd drop it or fold storm imagery into a broader "seascape"/"maritime" page if one gets built at all.
  • My claim about "dark floral wallpaper" was partly wrong for a specific reason worth remembering: most of what looks like strong demand for that phrase is actually demand for physical wall-covering wallpaper (Wayfair, Wallmur, home-decor blogs), not phone wallpaper. That's a different market entirely. If a floral page gets made, "moody floral" is closer to how phone-wallpaper sites and Etsy sellers phrase it, but the evidence is still weak, not strong.
  • "Dark academia" is confirmed as a real, multi-platform demand category, which validates it as the benchmark — good baseline for judging the others.
  • The most useful thing this search surfaced that wasn't on my original list: cottagecore, Renaissance aesthetic, and angelcore all show real dedicated-category evidence and, unlike storm/floral, are also a close visual match for what's actually in a museum-painting collection (pastoral/still-life, fresco/mythological, and Rococo cherub imagery respectively).
  • Seasonal themes (autumn/fall) have large category counts but the demand is time-boxed to a few months a year — worth building only if timed to launch before that window, not as a permanent page.
  • What I could not measure at all: actual Google/ChatGPT query volume (no keyword tool gave numbers without a paid account or login — Ahrefs, Semrush, Ubersuggest, and keywordtool.io free pages could not be reached at usable depth), Google Trends relative-interest charts (no API access, JS-rendered page returned no data via fetch), and Pinterest Trends' own numeric data (trends.pinterest.com is a JS app that returned no content via fetch). Reddit request-counting was also incomplete: reddit-buddy's search endpoint returned rate-limit and access errors for most of the session, so only two subreddits' top-of-year listings could actually be reviewed, and that data source (RSS fallback) doesn't expose upvote counts, so post volume/absence is the only thing that could be checked, not engagement.
